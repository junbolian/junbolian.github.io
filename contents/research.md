I study how a retailer should set prices and choose its assortment when demand data are limited, and how large language models can produce optimization models that are reliable enough to use. Most of this work is developed with, and tested at, Wenzhou Buyi Pharmacy Chain.

- **Retail pricing.** Most products in a large assortment have never had a price change, so their demand curves cannot be estimated. I study how to reprice such products, how to set store-level prices from sales recorded under a uniform price, and how a retailer should learn demand when competitors can see its online prices but not its in-store prices.
- **Assortment optimization.** I study which products a chain should keep or delist in a long-tail assortment, given substitution within groups of close products and coverage requirements.
- **LLMs for operations research.** I develop methods that verify optimization models written by language models (ReLoop), certify them without labeled answers (AdmitOR), and test whether language models can identify which constraints a Dantzig-Wolfe reformulation should group together (JointDW).
